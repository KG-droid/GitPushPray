# Pitch deck: speaker notes

Source slides are in `slides/` (Slides artifact format, `deck.json` gives the order). Slide notes below.

## 1. One journey from offer to Day 90

Open with the aim, not the features. Joining a bank takes weeks of vetting, and today the experience is a series of separate handovers: recruiter, vetting team, IT, manager, HR. Our idea is that the joiner should feel one continuous journey, from the moment they say yes to the point, around three months in, where they are part of the team. The app is a mobile-first companion for the joiner, with matching views for their manager and buddy. Everything you will see is built and running on a phone; all people and policies are fictional.

## 2. The silent gap before Day 1 is where journeys break

The problem is not the paperwork, it is the silence. Vetting for a bank takes weeks, and research (Speer, 2026) found each extra week between accepted offer and start raised the odds of a no-show by about 22%. Systems like Workday record joining but don’t guide anyone through it, so the joiner hears from a different team each time, or from nobody. Our aim is a single, coherent journey that carries someone from accepting the offer through Day 1, Day 30 and on to Day 90. We deliberately chose to measure connection and confidence, not task completion, because a joiner who has ticked every box but knows nobody has still not joined.

## 3. One thread through five moments

This is the heart of the pitch: the same app, the same thread, from before you join to Day 90. Before joining, the vetting tracker works like parcel tracking, with plain-English status and honest timings, and the joiner makes real choices such as laptop and benefits, and meets their manager and buddy. On Day 1 they get one plan rather than a pile of emails. In week 1 the app prompts the intros, training and first 1:1. At Day 30 a two-question pulse asks whether they know who to ask and whether they feel confident, and the manager sees the trend, never individual answers. Day 90 is where the journey points: goals agreed before Day 1, and a buddy tracker measured against Microsoft’s finding that 4 to 8 or more meetups in 90 days made the buddy genuinely useful. Today’s build is fully worked through Day 30, with Day 90 carried by goals, the buddy tracker and the handover to the team.

## 4. How it works, and why we designed it this way

Left side, how it works: one journey engine drives the joiner, manager, buddy and people-team views, so they are never out of step. Everything is an event, such as a check clearing, a laptop chosen or a goal set, and unlocks are rules over those events plus the joiner’s role, location and software. That gives chain reactions: when the manager sets goals, the welcome pack lands in the joiner’s app live. The assistant answers from policy with citations, knows the joiner’s own steps, and offers a person with a short summary when it is unsure or the topic is sensitive, such as pay. Right side, why: we show one next step because a 40-item checklist is overwhelming; locked items say what unlocks them so waiting feels earned; vetting is shown honestly with realistic timings but never results; and because start dates move in real life, the whole journey reflows when one slips. It is also privacy-minded: managers and buddies see signals and trends, never private chats.

## 5. Inclusion is built into the journey

Inclusion isn’t a separate page, it is part of the journey. Before Day 1 every joiner is asked, warmly and with three options (yes, not right now, or I’d prefer to talk to someone first), whether they need any adjustments. It is confidential: details go to IT and Occupational Health, and the manager only learns that a request was made. The answer also drives the journey, because laptop and headset choices wait for it, so the right equipment is ready on Day 1 rather than sorted weeks later. A jargon buster removes the barrier of unexplained banking acronyms. And because we built six very different personas, including a contact-centre advisor, branch colleague, graduate and a remote analyst, no one is handed a head-office template. The app is mobile-first with no extra login, and we designed for contrast, large touch targets and reduced motion. Close on the aim: connected and confident, from the first yes to Day 90.
